Assessment framework

The assignment is no longer the evidence

A finished product can hide who did the thinking. AI can also write reflections, show steps and script a defense. Keep the learning target; change what proves learning.

1

Prepare

Allow AI during learning: explore examples, practice, get feedback or test an argument.

2

Disrupt

Reveal a meaningful change: fresh evidence, a new condition or an important constraint.

3

Demonstrate

Ask students to act independently and show how they apply the same skill.

Five stronger design moves

1
Fresh evidence

A passage, source, dataset or example.

2
Changed constraint

A condition that alters the decision.

3
Decision with trade-offs

Defend why one choice is preferable.

4
Brief independent transfer

Apply the skill to an unseen case.

5
Visible checkpoints

Observe important thinking in class.

Cross-curricular examples

What it looks like in the classroom

Illustrative examples from the session, organized around the assessment architecture. Adapt them to your own learning targets.

Geometry

Can the ramp fit?

Prepare
Design a ramp with a 1:12 rise-to-run ratio, a 2 ft rise and 20 ft of straight run.
Disrupt
Reveal a side entrance with 28 ft available, including a 4 ft level landing.
Demonstrate
Calculate the remaining run, sketch to scale and defend whether it fits in three minutes.
Look for
Proportion, scale and justified decision. The simplified model leaves 24 ft of run; this is not a full accessibility-code review.
English 11

Revisit a claim with fresh text

Prepare
Argue whether Nick is a reliable narrator in The Great Gatsby using Chapters 1–3.
Disrupt
Reveal a later passage selected by the teacher.
Demonstrate
Point to a line, explain its effect and qualify the original thesis. Alternatively, compare two quotations and defend the stronger one.
Look for
Evidence-specific interpretation and reasoning.
U.S. History

Audit a source

Prepare
Evaluate whether the New Deal helped end the Depression using jobs statistics, a worker account and campaign material.
Disrupt
Reveal that the campaign source was designed to persuade voters, not measure employment.
Demonstrate
In 90 seconds, explain what the source is evidence of and what needs corroboration.
Look for
Distinguish author argument from evidence of economic outcomes.
Biology

Distinguish explanation from test

Prepare
Explain how temperature affects catalase activity: it rises to an optimum, then falls sharply.
Disrupt
Ask whether pH or measurement error could explain the drop.
Demonstrate
Propose a controlled follow-up test.
Look for
Connect the proposed test to the competing explanation.
Visual Arts

Defend a design decision

Prepare
Create an event poster and artist statement.
Disrupt
Require adaptation for low-vision viewers.
Demonstrate
Revise the poster and explain hierarchy, contrast and trade-offs.
Look for
Design decisions tied to the new audience.
Computer Science

Test the assumption

Prepare
Develop a Python program that calculates means from CSV data.
Disrupt
Introduce blanks, outliers and invalid inputs.
Demonstrate
Verify the program handles the new cases and justify chosen behavior.
Look for
Correct handling and reasoning about data assumptions.

Teacher · Geometry

Make the math task harder to outsource

I teach high school geometry. Students are designing a ramp using a 1:12 maximum rise-to-run ratio. Assume they can paste the entire assignment into Gemini. Suggest one realistic site constraint to reveal after the initial design. Create a 3-minute independent task requiring a scale sketch and a mathematical justification. Keep the mathematics accessible.
Teacher · Stress test

Prompt Gemini to attack your design

Act as a skeptical instructional designer. Learning target: [target]. Assignment: [task]. Assume a student can paste the entire task, rubric and all source materials into Gemini. Identify which products fail to establish independent understanding. Propose two brief, accessible in-class performances that assess the same target, explain why each is stronger evidence, and flag any unnecessary workload.
Teacher · Transfer

Prompt Gemini to create meaningful variation

Given this learning target [target] and original assignment [task], propose three transfer variations. Each must preserve the target, alter a conceptually important condition, include what a proficient student would demonstrate, and avoid trick wording. Label the cognitive demand of each variation.
Student · Challenge

Prompt Gemini to act as a skeptic

You are my skeptical peer, not my ghostwriter. I will give my claim, evidence and criteria. Challenge one important assumption and identify what additional evidence could change the decision. Ask only one question at a time. Do not write the final response or invent evidence.
Student · Source audit

Prompt Gemini to audit a source

Review the source excerpt and my interpretation. Separate direct quotation, defensible inference, and unsupported assertion. Identify provenance questions and one corroborating source type I should seek. Never invent a citation or claim a source was verified when it was not.

Attendee practice

Redesign one assignment

Choose a task students already submit. Assume Gemini completes it perfectly. Add one independent evidence check. The session activity allows eight minutes to work and four minutes to share.

Five-minute setup

  1. Name the skill: what thinking is the target?
  2. Allow practice: where can Gemini help?
  3. Hold back one element: what will be newly introduced?
  4. Require one decision: what choice must be defended?

When to check

During work: stop and predict before testing. After a draft: introduce an unseen piece. At the end: use a short independent transfer task.

Assessment guidance

Look for evidence, not extra work

Accuracy

Does the response fit the new evidence?

Reasoning

Can the student justify a choice?

Transfer

Can they use the concept again?

Avoid

  • Longer essays
  • Mandatory AI transcripts
  • Generic reflections
  • AI detectors as proof

Prefer

  • A short meaningful variation
  • One observed choice
  • Evidence-specific explanation
  • Repeated low-stakes checks
The assignment can be AI-assisted. The understanding cannot be assumed.

Design one moment where students must show what they know. No single check guarantees authorship; use accessible, target-aligned evidence over time.
